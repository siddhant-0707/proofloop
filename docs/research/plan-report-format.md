# How real US emergency plans are formatted (for the ProofLoop plan report)

Researched 2026-10-24. Primary sources only: FEMA (fema.gov, training.fema.gov), Florida Division of Emergency Management (floridadisaster.org), Florida Administrative Code (flrules.org), and Taylor and Dixie County sites. Page numbers are the printed page numbers in each document unless noted.

## Summary

- Local US plans follow FEMA's **CPG 101 v3 (2021)**. A plan is a **base plan** plus **annexes**. The base plan has ten fixed parts: introductory material; purpose, scope, situation overview and planning assumptions; concept of operations; organization and assignment of responsibilities; direction, control and coordination; information collection, analysis and dissemination; communications; administration, finance and logistics; plan development and maintenance; authorities and references.
- Evacuation and sheltering usually sit in **functional annexes** ("protective actions", "mass care", "public alert and warning") or a **hurricane annex**. FEMA's 2019 evacuation guide adds zones, five phases, critical transportation needs, clearance time, shelter demand and re-entry.
- An **Incident Action Plan (IAP)** is a different thing. It covers one operational period (12 to 24 hours) during a response. Its core forms are **ICS 202** (objectives) and **ICS 204** (assignment lists), with 203, 205, 205A, 206, 207 and 208 often attached.
- Florida counties write a **Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan (CEMP)** under Rule 27P-6. It must include an evacuation component and a shelter component. Real county CEMPs (Taylor County) use Introduction / Concept of Operations / Method of Operations / Financial Management / References and Authorities.
- ProofLoop's output is best framed as a **hurricane evacuation and shelter plan** using CPG 101 base-plan headings, with **ICS 202 and ICS 204 style attachments**. Stress-test results fit CPG 101's "courses of action", "capability estimate" and plan-review criteria. The full outline is at the end.

### 1.1 What a base plan is for

- The base plan gives an overview of the program, states policies, describes the response organization and assigns tasks. Its main audience is the senior official, staff, agency heads and the community. ([CPG 101, "Creating an EOP Base Plan", p. 67](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- The most common format is "function-focused". It has three parts: base plan, functional annexes, and hazard-specific annexes. ([CPG 101, §2.1 Function-Focused Format, p. 31](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- A CEMP can be the jurisdiction's EOP. In other places it is broader program guidance. State law may prescribe its contents. ([CPG 101, §4 Additional Types of Plans, pp. 37–40](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))

### 1.3 Annexes: functional and hazard-specific

- Annexes add detail beyond the base plan. Common ones cover functions (for example emergency sheltering) and hazards (for example earthquakes). ([CPG 101, "Adding EOP Annexes", p. 81](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- Functional annexes describe policies, processes, roles and responsibilities for one function. They should build on the base plan, not repeat it. Jurisdictions may align them to the Emergency Support Functions (ESFs). ([CPG 101, §1 Functional Annexes and §1.1, pp. 81–82](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Mass Care checklist** (§1.1.10): how to identify, open and staff shelters; equitable service; life-sustaining goods; pets and service animals; coordination with expected numbers evacuated; feeding; reunification; arrangements with other jurisdictions when local sheltering is not practical; ADA site selection and accessibility. ([CPG 101, Functional Annexes Content, pp. 81–102](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Protective Actions checklist** (§1.1.13, covers evacuation and shelter-in-place): criteria and authority to order evacuation; how to conduct it; early evacuation for people with mobility issues; receiving evacuees; tracking; return of evacuees; options when people refuse to evacuate; "sufficient, timely and accessible transportation" for people without their own; collecting and tracking transportation requests; identifying accessible vehicles. ([CPG 101, pp. 81–102](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Public Alert and Warning checklist** (§1.1.14): how to send warnings, explicitly including "door-to-door warnings", WEA, EAS and social media; how to reach people with sensory or cognitive disabilities; include draft public messages. ([CPG 101, pp. 81–102](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Hazard-specific annexes** usually identify risk areas, evacuation routes, warning protocols and protective measures. They may include maps, tables and checklists. Their concept of operations covers hazard assessment, public warnings, selection and implementation of protective actions, short-term stabilization, and recovery. ([CPG 101, §2 Hazard- or Threat-Specific Annexes, p. 102](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- The **Hurricanes/Severe Storm Annex** covers the jurisdiction's concerns, capabilities, agencies and resources, and must include a hazard analysis summary of where and how hurricanes hit. ([CPG 101, §4.6, p. 105](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))

### 1.4 The six planning steps and courses of action

The six steps are: (1) Form a Collaborative Planning Team, (2) Understand the Situation, (3) Determine Goals and Objectives, (4) Develop the Plan, (5) Prepare and Review the Plan, (6) Implement and Maintain the Plan. ([CPG 101, "The Planning Process", pp. 43–66](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))

- **Step 3** separates goals (broad outcomes) from objectives (specific actions). EOP objectives differ from incident objectives, which an incident commander sets for an operational period. Some hazard annexes include "suggested incident or EOC objectives for the initial operational periods". ([CPG 101, §1.3, pp. 52–53](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Step 4, Develop and Analyze Courses of Action**: generate, compare and select solutions. Teams "should always consider at least two options". Each course of action answers what, who, when, where, why and how, and is checked for support of priorities, feasibility and acceptability. Steps: estimate a timeline (a hurricane's onset is "typically days"), identify decision points, identify operational tasks. Pause to find "single points of failure" and gaps. Then compare costs and benefits and select. ([CPG 101, §1.4.1, pp. 53–57](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Capability estimate**: an assessment of whether a course of action is realistic and supportable. It names criteria, facts and assumptions, and the risks of a course of action. The plan must account for "unsolvable resource shortfalls". ([CPG 101, §1.4.2, pp. 53–57](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **External review of courses of action**: outside experts challenge assumptions and look for errors in assumptions or resources. ([CPG 101, §1.4.2, pp. 53–57](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Step 5, writing rules**: plain English, checklists and maps, short sentences, active voice. ([CPG 101, §1.5.1, p. 57](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Step 5, review criteria**: adequacy, feasibility, acceptability, completeness, compliance. Feasible means the mission can be done "with available resources within the time contemplated". Acceptable includes adhering "to cost and time constraints". Complete includes time estimates, success criteria and a desired end state. ([CPG 101, §1.5.2, pp. 57–61](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- **Step 6**: train, exercise, review and revise. ([CPG 101, §1.6, pp. 61–66](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))

### 1.5 How CPG 101 separates an EOP from an IAP

- IAPs are "iterative operational plans" written before each operational period, typically every 12 or 24 hours. They list the incident commander's objectives and the tactics and resources for that period. Good EOPs guide the first IAPs. ([CPG 101, §4 Additional Types of Plans, pp. 37–40](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))
- Glossary: an IAP is "an oral or written plan containing the objectives established by the incident commander or unified command and addressing tactics and support activities for the planned operational period, generally 12 to 24 hours." ([CPG 101, Appendix C: Glossary](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf))

## 2. The ICS Incident Action Plan and its forms

Sources: FEMA, *NIMS ICS Forms Booklet*, FEMA 502-2, September 2010 ([training.fema.gov PDF](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf)); the current [ICS Forms page](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/icsforms/) at the EMI ICS Resource Center, which lists ICS 202 and ICS 204 at version 3.1; and FEMA, *Incident Action Planning Guide*, Revision 1, July 2015 ([fema.gov PDF](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf)).

### 2.1 Which forms make up an IAP

FEMA 502-2 marks forms "typically included in an IAP" with one asterisk, and forms that "could be used in the IAP" with two. ([FEMA 502-2, "ICS Forms List"](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf))

| Form | Title | IAP status in FEMA 502-2 | Typically prepared by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICS 201 | Incident Briefing | Not starred; "can serve as part of the initial IAP" | Initial Incident Commander |
| ICS 202 | Incident Objectives | Typically included | Planning Section Chief |
| ICS 203 | Organization Assignment List | Typically included | Resources Unit Leader |
| ICS 204 | Assignment List | Typically included | Resources Unit Leader and Operations Section Chief |
| ICS 205 | Incident Radio Communications Plan | Typically included | Communications Unit Leader |
| ICS 205A | Communications List | Could be used | Communications Unit Leader |
| ICS 206 | Medical Plan | Typically included | Medical Unit Leader |
| ICS 207 | Incident Organization Chart | Not starred, but listed in the ICS 202 IAP checklist | Resources Unit Leader |
| ICS 208 | Safety Message/Plan | Could be used | Safety Officer |
| ICS 215 | Operational Planning Worksheet | Not in IAP; used to build the ICS 204s | Operations Section Chief |
| ICS 215A | Incident Action Plan Safety Analysis | Not in IAP; planning worksheet | Safety Officer |

- ICS 202 block 6 is the IAP checklist. Its options are ICS 203, 204, 205, 205A, 206, 207, 208, "Map/Chart", "Weather Forecast/Tides/Currents" and "Other Attachments". ([ICS 202 v3.1](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/ics%20form%20202,%20incident%20objectives%20(v3.1).pdf))
- ICS 215 records required, have and need for each resource type. The Resources Unit uses it to complete the ICS 204s. ([FEMA 502-2, ICS 215 instructions](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf))
- ICS 215A lists incident areas, hazards and risks, and mitigations. ([FEMA 502-2, ICS 215A instructions](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf))
- FEMA's own assembly order for an IAP (Table 7): cover sheet; ICS 202; ICS 205; ICS 205A; ICS 207; incident map; ICS 204; ICS 220 (air ops, if needed); ICS 206; ICS 230 meeting schedule; ICS 208 (optional); ICS 213 (optional); other components. This guide is written for FEMA-led field operations, so local teams may differ. ([IAP Guide, Table 7, p. 35](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf))
- NIMS defines an IAP as oral or written. FEMA requires a written one. ([IAP Guide, Ch. 2, footnote 1, p. 3](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf))

### 2.2 ICS 202, Incident Objectives: fields

Purpose: "describes the basic incident strategy, incident objectives, command emphasis/priorities, and safety considerations for use during the next operational period." It "can be used as the opening or cover page" of the IAP. ([ICS 202 v3.1](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/ics%20form%20202,%20incident%20objectives%20(v3.1).pdf); [FEMA 502-2](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf))

| Block | Title | Instruction (short) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident Name | Name of the incident; an incident number can be added. |
| 2 | Operational Period | Start and end date and time (24-hour clock). |
| 3 | Objective(s) | Clear, concise objectives, ideally in priority order. Use the SMART model: Specific, Measurable, Action-oriented, Realistic, Time-sensitive. |
| 4 | Operational Period Command Emphasis; General Situational Awareness | Where to place emphasis, such as tactical priorities or order of events. Not a narrative. Situational awareness may include weather, conditions and a safety message. |
| 5 | Site Safety Plan Required? | Yes or No, and where approved site safety plans are kept. |
| 6 | Incident Action Plan | Checkboxes for the forms and attachments included in this IAP. |
| 7 | Prepared by | Name, position/title, signature, date/time. |
| 8 | Approved by Incident Commander | Name, signature, date/time. |

FEMA practice adds: number objectives once for the whole incident, list them in priority order, and give a modified objective a sub-letter (2 becomes 2a). ([IAP Guide, after Table 4, p. 24](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf)) The guide also defines the chain Priorities → Objectives → Strategies → Tactics/Work Assignments, where tactics say "who, what, where, and when" and are recorded on ICS 215 then ICS 204. ([IAP Guide, Table 4, p. 23](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf))

### 2.3 ICS 204, Assignment List: fields

Purpose: tells Division and Group supervisors their assignments. Prepared by the Resources Unit from the ICS 202, ICS 215 and the Operations Section Chief. Approved by the Incident Commander. One ICS 204 per Division or Group. ([ICS 204 v3.1](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/ics%20form%20204,%20assignment%20list%20(v3.1).pdf); [FEMA 502-2](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20forms/nims%20ics%20forms%20booklet%20(v3).pdf))

| Block | Title | Instruction (short) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident Name | Name of the incident. |
| 2 | Operational Period | Start and end date and time. |
| 3 | Branch / Division / Group / Staging Area | Short labels such as "Branch 1", "Division D". |
| 4 | Operations Personnel | Names and contact numbers of the Operations Section Chief, Branch Director and Division/Group Supervisor. |
| 5 | Resources Assigned | Resource identifier (use "TBD" if not yet known), leader, number of persons, contact, and notes: reporting location and time, special equipment, transportation needs. |
| 6 | Work Assignments | "A statement of the tactical objectives to be achieved within the operational period." |
| 7 | Special Instructions | Safety problems, precautions, drop-off or pick-up points, other key information. |
| 8 | Communications | Radio and phone contacts for this assignment, from the ICS 205. |
| 9 | Prepared by | Name, position/title, signature, date/time. |

FEMA's ICS 204 rules: organize 204s geographically if the incident is organized by area. Specify exactly which resource does each job. "Do not include work assignments that are not in direct support of incident objectives." "Do not leave blocks blank"; use "TBD", "N/A" or "UNK". ([IAP Guide, Table 8, p. 36](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Incident_Action_Planning_Guide_Revision1_august2015.pdf))

## 3. Evacuation and shelter planning specifics

### 3.1 FEMA, *Planning Considerations: Evacuation and Shelter-in-Place* (July 2019)

Source: [fema.gov PDF](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf). It builds on CPG 101 and adds evacuation-specific content, job aids and checklists (p. 1).

**Definitions (p. 1).** Evacuation is the "organized, phased, and supervised withdrawal, dispersal, or removal of civilians from dangerous or potentially dangerous areas, and their reception and care in safe areas." Shelter-in-place is "the use of a structure to temporarily separate individuals from a hazard or threat."

**Jurisdiction duties (pp. 2–3).** The local government issues evacuation orders, manages traffic, identifies routes, identifies shelters (including in other communities), and creates pre-approved accessible messaging. It should establish and test a community warning system.

**Key concepts (pp. 4–9).**
- Zones: "move as few people as needed the shortest distance to safety." Zones reduce resource burden and speed re-entry (p. 4).
- Transportation models: hub-and-spoke and point-to-point. Plans should cover staging, embarkation points, transportation centers and shelter locations (p. 4).
- Five phases: Mobilization; Evacuation and Shelter-in-Place; Impact (includes "zero hour", when responders must stop field work); Displacement/Mass Care; Re-entry (pp. 5–6).
- Types of evacuees, including Critical Transportation Needs (CTN) evacuees: people without transportation who need help to leave, also called transportation-dependent (pp. 7–9).
- Evacuation facilities: assembly points, respite sites, regional hub reception centers, and shelters (pp. 8–9).

**Critical considerations (pp. 10–18).** These are the topics a plan should address:
- Accessibility (p. 10); children and unaccompanied minors (p. 10).
- Evacuation clearance time: from the order until the last evacuee leaves or remaining people must shelter in place. Include people with access and functional needs and pass-through evacuees (p. 12).
- Fuel management (p. 12); homeless populations, including outreach teams that notify and transport people (p. 13).
- Hospitals and residential medical facilities (p. 14); household pets and service animals (p. 14).
- Individuals with access and functional needs (p. 15); legal requirements and authorities (p. 16).
- Mass care and sheltering services (p. 16); population assessment (p. 16).
- Terminology in public messaging: the public may read "mandatory", "voluntary" or "phased" differently than officials. Plan alternate ways to reach people missed by normal warning systems (p. 17).
- Tracking and evacuee accountability (p. 17); traffic management (p. 18).
- Zonal approach: zones help estimate network capacity, critical transportation needs, clearance times and shelter demand (p. 18).

**Planning principles (pp. 22–25).** Defined authorities; shelter-in-place as first/default option; common understanding; unified coordination; zone-based operational strategies; all-hazard planning; whole community; flexibility and scalability; Critical Transportation Needs ("plans should account for CTN populations", p. 24); service animals and pets; evacuee tracking; mass care (assign evacuees to shelters before they leave an embarkation point, p. 25).

**Job aids and checklists (Appendices D and E).**
- Command Job Aid: decision questions at 120, 96, 72, 48 and 24 hours before impact, then at impact, mass care and re-entry. Examples: "What is the capacity of the receiving zone to accept evacuees?" and "What is the status of mass care shelters?" (pp. 34–36).
- Essential Elements of Information table: element, timeline, responsible entity, specific information, source. Examples: evacuation order timing, incident area boundaries, shelters open or full (pp. 37–42).
- Execution checklists by ESF: ESF #1 Transportation, ESF #5 Information and Planning, ESF #6 Mass Care (pp. 43–49).

**Six-step process applied to evacuation (Appendix G, pp. 57–59).** An evacuation/shelter-in-place annex should cover what the EOP does not already cover. Courses of action should include resource allocation and prioritization. Plans should aim to reduce the number of people who must evacuate, shelter evacuees as close as safely possible, pick shelters not exposed to the hazard, and coordinate competing demands for the same resources.

### 3.2 ESF #6, Mass Care, Emergency Assistance, Temporary Housing, and Human Services Annex (June 2016)

Source: [fema.gov PDF](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/fema_ESF_6_Mass-Care.pdf). This is the federal NRF annex. Local and state annexes often mirror it.

- **Structure.** Introduction (Purpose, Scope); Relationship to Whole Community; Core Capabilities and Actions. (ESF #6-1 to #6-8)
- **Four functions** (ESF #6-1 to #6-2): Mass Care (congregate sheltering, feeding, emergency supplies, reunification); Emergency Assistance (includes support to mass evacuations, people with access and functional needs, and pets and service animals); Temporary Housing; Human Services.
- **Sheltering** means "life-sustaining services in congregate facilities that provide a safe, sanitary, and secure environment". It also covers support to people sheltering in place. (ESF #6-6)
- **Mass Evacuee Support** includes tracking the movement of evacuees, pets, service animals and medical equipment. (ESF #6-6)
- **Logistics** under ESF #6 means gathering, prioritizing and communicating resource requirements. (ESF #6-8)
- Local governments are first responsible. Federal help comes when needs exceed local and state capability. (ESF #6-1, #6-4)

### 3.3 Elements an evacuation and shelter plan should contain (combined list)

Pulling the three FEMA sources together, the recurring elements are:

1. Authority to order evacuation and the decision criteria ([CPG 101 §1.1.13](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf); [Evacuation guide p. 16, p. 22](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf)).
2. Population at risk by zone, with access and functional needs counts ([Evacuation guide pp. 16, 18](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf)).
3. Transportation-dependent (CTN) population and the transport resources assigned to them ([Evacuation guide pp. 7–9, 24](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf); [CPG 101 §1.1.13](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf)).
4. Shelter locations, capacity and shelter demand, including out-of-area options ([Evacuation guide pp. 2, 18](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf); [CPG 101 §1.1.10](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf)).
5. Public warning and information, including door-to-door warnings and plain-language messages ([CPG 101 §1.1.14](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_cpg-101-v3-developing-maintaining-eops.pdf); [Evacuation guide p. 17](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/planning-considerations-evacuation-and-shelter-in-place.pdf)).
6. ## 4. Florida practice: state rules, state CEMP, and Taylor and Dixie counties

### 4.1 State rule for county plans

- Rule 27P-6.0023, Florida Administrative Code, effective 7/18/2013 ([flrules.org](https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleNo.asp?id=27P-6.0023); rule text read via the [Cornell LII mirror](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-27P-6-0023)):
  - The county plan "will include an evacuation component, a shelter component (risk and host events), and a post-disaster and recovery component" (subsection 2).
  - Responsibilities "must be assigned by position title or agency name". Checklists, maps and diagrams are encouraged (subsection 3).
  - The plan has at least two parts: the **Basic Plan** (narrative, with Recovery and Mitigation annexes) and the **Capability Assessment** (subsection 4).
  - The plan must "identify and describe pre-emergency warning systems, evacuation and sheltering plans" (subsection 6).
  - Plans are reviewed against forms CEMP-001 and CEMP-002 (subsection 7).
- **CEMP-001 compliance crosswalk** (2020 edition, [xlsx on floridadisaster.org](https://www.floridadisaster.org/globalassets/cemp/2020-cemp/2020-local-cemp-criteria_cemp001.xlsx)). Its Basic Plan sheet uses: I. Introduction (I.A Purpose, I.B Scope, I.C Methodology); II. Situation (II.A Hazards Analysis, II.B Geographic Info, II.C Demographics, II.D Economic Profile); III. Concept of Operations (III.A Organization, with an ESF lead/support matrix); preparedness activities (including special-needs registration and evacuation zone maps); V. Financial Management; VI. References and Authorities. Then Annex I: Recovery and Annex II: Mitigation. The Executive Summary sheet says the Basic Plan covers "direction and control, organizational structure, alert notification and warning" and the four phases.
- **2026 County CEMP Guidance Fact Sheet** ([PDF](https://www.floridadisaster.org/globalassets/dem/response/planning-section/cemp/fact-sheet-_county-cemp-guidance.pdf)). After 2025 changes to Chapter 252, F.S., FDEM provides templates: a County Base Plan template ("purpose, scope, methodology, roles and responsibilities, emergency phases, and concept of operations"), an ESF Annex template, a Natural Hazard Annex template, and a **Shelter Plan Annex template**. Counties are still reviewed against the crosswalk.

### 4.2 State of Florida CEMP (revised 2026)

Source: [State CEMP 2026 PDF](https://www.floridadisaster.org/globalassets/1/2026-state-of-florida-cemp-4-8-26.pdf), table of contents pp. 2–4.

- Top-level sections: Introduction (Purpose; Relationship to Other Plans; Situations and Assumptions: Geography, Demographics, Hazards, Planning Assumptions); Roles and Responsibilities (Direction and Control, SERT, ESFs); Operating Facility; Concept of Operations (Preparedness Activities; Monitoring, Detection, Alert and Warning; Emergency Declaration; Activation and Notification; Response Operations; Recovery Operations; Mitigation Measures); Financial Management; Plan Development and Maintenance; Legal Considerations; References and Authorities; Acronyms.
- "Emergency Protective Measures" has two subsections, **Evacuations** and **Sheltering** (pp. 30–31). Executive Order 80-29 lets counties order evacuations and open shelters, including special needs and pet-friendly shelters. Shelters must meet ADA and Florida accessibility rules. FDEM uses the American Red Cross *Standards for Hurricane Shelter Selection* as minimum safety criteria. Counties with designated shelters must also designate a pet-friendly shelter (s. 252.3568, F.S.).
- Florida numbers its ESFs differently from the federal list. **ESF 14 is External Affairs** and ESF 15 is Volunteers and Donations; there are 20 state ESFs ([FDEM CEMP page](https://www.floridadisaster.org/dem/response/planning-section/all-hazards/comprehensive-emergency-management-plan/)). Federally, public information is ESF #15.
- The **Florida ESF 6 Mass Care Annex** (2024, [PDF](https://www.floridadisaster.org/globalassets/2024-cemp---esf-6.pdf)) is titled in this pattern: Section 1 Introduction (1.1 Purpose, 1.2 Scope, 1.3 Assumptions); Section 2 Roles and Responsibilities (2.1 Direction and Control, 2.2 Primary Agency, 2.3 Support Agencies); Section 3 Concept of Operations (3.1 Preparedness, 3.2 Activation and Notification, 3.3 Response Operations, 3.4 Recovery Operations); Section 4 Finance and Administration (4.1 Financial Management, 4.2 Administration, 4.3 Authorities and References).

### 4.3 Taylor County CEMP

- **2016 CEMP** (text-searchable; [PDF](https://cms6.revize.com/revize/taylorcounty/CEMP_2016.pdf), linked from the [county CEMP page](https://www.taylorcountygov.com/residents/comprehensive_em_plan.php)). Table of contents (p. 4):

  - III. Method of Operation (A. General, including Emergency Operations and Joint Information Center; B. Coordination, Direction and Control; C. Prevention; D. Preparedness, including 3. Plan Development and Maintenance, 4. Public Information, 6. **Evacuation**, 7. **Sheltering**; E. Response)
  - IV. Financial Management; V. Continuity of Operations; VI. References and Authorities
  - Then a Recovery Annex and a Mitigation Annex, then a hazard and vulnerability analysis.
- The 2016 plan's preparedness objectives include "Shelter Deficit Objectives" and "Evacuation Objectives" (pp. 46–47). The Sheltering section cites the special needs registry under s. 252.355, F.S., and uses Red Cross ARC 4496 as the minimum shelter safety standard (p. 53). The Evacuation section describes the state Regional Evacuation Procedures (p. 52).
- **2025 CEMP** ([PDF](https://www.taylorcountygov.com/2025%20CEMP.pdf), dated "Nov. 2025", linked as current from the [Emergency Management page](https://www.taylorcountygov.com/departments/emergency_management.php)). It is a 132-page scanned image with no text layer. Only the first pages were checked visually. ## Recommended report outline for ProofLoop

**Framing.** ProofLoop's plan is a pre-landfall plan for one storm. It is not a full county CEMP, and it is not a full IAP. The closest real document is a **hazard-specific annex**, here an evacuation and shelter annex for Hurricane Isaias. Use the CPG 101 base-plan headings, which Florida plans also follow, and add two IAP-style attachments in ICS 202 and ICS 204 format. CPG 101 supports this: hazard annexes may include "suggested incident … objectives for the initial operational periods" (§1.3, p. 53).

**Labels.** Mark every page "EXERCISE – SIMULATED PLAN – NOT AN OFFICIAL COUNTY DOCUMENT". Do not invent signatures or a promulgation statement. Where a real plan needs a fact ProofLoop does not have (names, radio channels, agencies), print "TBD", following the FEMA ICS 204 rule never to leave a block blank (IAP Guide, Table 8).

ProofLoop field names below refer to `backend/src/proofloop/loop/schemas.py` (`Plan`, `SimulationResult`, `Assignment`, `NeighborhoodBriefing`, `Evidence`, `StressTest`, `Candidate`).

| # | Section | Real-plan basis | What ProofLoop puts there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cover Page and Record of Changes | CPG 101 §1 Introductory Material | Title ("Hurricane Isaias Evacuation and Shelter Plan – Dixie and Taylor Counties" or the town name), date, jurisdiction, the exercise marking. The record of changes lists planner/adversary rounds: round number, the attack applied, and what changed. |
| 1 | Purpose | CPG 101 §2.1; CEMP-001 I.A | One paragraph: protect residents from Isaias flooding by opening shelters, staging high-water transport, warning people outside FEMA flood zones, and holding a reserve. |
| 2 | Scope | CPG 101 §2.2; CEMP-001 I.B | Communities covered (`Scenario.scope`), activation conditions, and what is out of scope (medical evacuation, pets, re-entry timing). |
| 3 | Situation Overview | CPG 101 §2.3; CEMP-001 II; State CEMP "Situations and Assumptions" | Per community: people affected, people outside FEMA flood zones, closed roads, offline shelters. Include the hazard map. Subsection **3.1 Hazard Analysis Summary**: NISAR flood extent versus FEMA flood zones. |
| 4 | Planning Assumptions | CPG 101 §2.4 | Budget (`Scenario.budget`), shelter capacities, and model assumptions from `Evidence.assumptions`. State plainly that these are the facts assumed to make the plan executable. |
| 5 | Concept of Operations | CPG 101 §3; hazard annex CONOPS (p. 102); evacuation guide phases (pp. 5–6) | The intent and end state in a few sentences. Then the actions in phase order: Mobilization (open shelters, fund reserve), Evacuation and Shelter-in-Place (stage transport, door-to-door outreach), Mass Care (shelter operations), Re-entry (marked "not modeled"). Subsections: **5.1 Protective Actions** (transport and outreach), **5.2 Mass Care and Sheltering** (shelters, capacity, people assigned per `ShelterAssignment`). |
| 6 | Organization and Assignment of Responsibilities | CPG 101 §4; Rule 27P-6.0023(3) | A primary/support matrix by ESF: ESF 1 Transportation (transport seats), ESF 6 Mass Care (shelters), ESF 14 External Affairs in Florida's numbering (outreach and warnings), ESF 5 Information and Planning, ESF 7 Resource Management (reserve). Agencies are "TBD". |
| 7 | Direction, Control and Coordination | CPG 101 §5 | Short standard text: the county EOC coordinates and the two counties coordinate through the state. Mark as "to be completed by the jurisdiction". Keep it so the report has the expected shape. |
| 8 | Information Collection, Analysis and Dissemination | CPG 101 §6 ("best provided as a table"); evacuation guide EEI table (pp. 37–42) | A table of information elements: flood extent (NISAR), flood zones (FEMA NFHL), population, shelter status, road status. Columns: source, who uses it, when it is needed. Fill from `Evidence.sources`. |
| 9 | Communications: Public Alert and Warning | CPG 101 §7 and §1.1.14; evacuation guide p. 17 | Which communities get door-to-door outreach and why (people outside FEMA zones). Plain-language draft message. Reaching people the normal alerts miss. |
| 10 | Administration, Finance and Logistics | CPG 101 §8 (8.2 Finance, 8.3 Logistics); CEMP-001 V | **Finance**: cost per action, total cost, contingency reserve, unfunded actions (`SimulationResult.cost`, `unfunded_actions`). **Logistics**: transport seats and shelter spaces, as required / have / need in the style of the ICS 215 columns. |
| 11 | Plan Development and Maintenance | CPG 101 §9, Step 4 (courses of action, capability estimate, external review) and Step 5 (review criteria) | **11.1 Courses of Action Considered**: the `Candidate` plans, with the selected one and why. **11.2 Plan Validation (Stress Test)**: results under shelter outage, road closure, budget cut and warning failure (`StressTest.cases`), stated as single points of failure and shortfalls. **11.3 Review Against CPG 101 Criteria**: feasibility, acceptability (cost), completeness (`Verdict.checks`). **11.4 Update Triggers**: new imagery or forecast. |
| 12 | Authorities and References | CPG 101 §10; CEMP-001 VI | Chapter 252, F.S.; Rule 27P-6; CPG 101; the FEMA evacuation guide; and data sources (`PlanReport.references`). CPG 101 says to list "computer software" used to build the plan, so name ProofLoop and its models here. |
| Tab A | Incident Objectives (ICS 202 format) | FEMA 502-2 ICS 202; IAP Guide Table 4 | Incident name, operational period (pre-landfall), numbered SMART objectives in priority order (for example "Shelter N people in M shelters by H-24"), command emphasis, and an attachments checklist. Prepared-by and approved-by are "TBD". |
| Tab B | Assignment Lists (ICS 204 format), one per community | FEMA 502-2 ICS 204; IAP Guide Table 8 | One sheet per community, treated as a Division: resources assigned (shelter, transport seats, outreach team), work assignment (from `Assignment.work` and `NeighborhoodBriefing.steps`), special instructions (road closed, shelter offline), communications "TBD". |
| Tab C | Maps | CPG 101 §2.3.1 (maps of high-risk areas); ICS 202 "Map/Chart" | Hazard map: flood extent, flood zones, shelters, communities. |
| Tab D | Methodology, Data Sources and Limitations | CPG 101 §2.3.1 ("assumptions and methods" of the hazard analysis); §1.4.2 ("unsolvable resource shortfalls") | Per figure: formula, inputs, assumptions and limitations (`Evidence`, `PlanReport.limitations`). Include people still unreached. |

**Sections deliberately left out.** Promulgation and approval pages, continuity of operations, recovery and mitigation annexes. Real CEMPs have them, but ProofLoop has no data for them, and producing them would imply official status.
